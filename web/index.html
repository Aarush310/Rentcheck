Before you pay anything

  1. View it in person and make sure the keys open the locks before handing over any money.
  2. Check who you're dealing with. Use recognised letting agencies or people whose identity you can verify. If answers are vague, walk away.
  3. Pay in a way you can trace, ideally a credit card. Never cash, money-transfer services, crypto or gift cards.
  4. Already paid? Contact your bank straight away and report it to your local Garda station.

Advice from An Garda Síochána's National Economic Crime Bureau, August 2026. Gardaí received over 230 reports of rental and reservation scams in January–July 2026, with reported losses of over €400,000 (provisional).

Questions to send the advertiser

Hi, I'm interested in the property. Before going further: 1. When can I view it in person? 2. Are you the owner or an agent? Can you show photo ID at the viewing? 3. Is the tenancy registered with the RTB? 4. How should the deposit be paid, and when? I'll only pay after viewing and signing a lease.

What the rent figures mean

Source: RTB Average Monthly Rent Report, published by the CSO as table RIQ02 and listed on data.gov.ie (CC-BY 4.0). The figures are average rents agreed in newly registered tenancies. They are not asking prices, and advertised rents are usually higher. The RTB withholds figures for small samples, so some areas have no figure for every size or type, and it publishes nothing for rooms in shared homes.

This is a stored snapshot, not a live feed. Each figure shows its own quarter; where the newest quarter has no figure, the latest of the previous four is used. Map positions are approximate area centres, not boundaries. RentCheck does not use live listings: sites such as Daft don't openly license their data and their terms don't allow scraping.
